My Story

The Work Has Always Been the Same

I have spent more than thirty years walking into rooms that needed changing and leaving them better than I found them. From construction sites around the globe to corporate boardrooms, to youth programs in Houston, the work has always been the same: find the genius in the room and give it permission to show up.

I discovered my life's purpose in my thirties by doing something simple: I looked back at every job I'd ever held and asked what I loved most and what I did best. The answer was always the same: developing people. I named it "Unleashing Genius" and have been proving it ever since.

In July 2026, I retired from Bechtel after a rewarding career as a leadership development and personal growth coach and mentor, workforce effectiveness operations manager, and workshop facilitator. I moved to Florida to reconnect with my youngest brother and build the next chapter: three tracks of coaching work built around the people and communities I've always been heading toward.

Along the way, I found myself in rooms most people never see. At Covenant House, I sat with young people the system had already written off, listening long after everyone else had given up on them. A former colleague and close friend showed me a different kind of room: the inside of a correctional facility, where he was having the conversations most people are afraid to have. What he taught me never left me, and I'm building a program now to carry that work forward. Somewhere between those two worlds, I was also in boardrooms with C-suite executives who had everything except the one thing that actually moves people: someone willing to tell them the truth.

None of that came easy. I'm no stranger to adversity myself. I learned at a young age just how powerful the mind is, that you can use it to move forward, or let it turn you into a victim. I've always chosen the former, and that choice shapes everything I do with the people I work with.

The thread running through all of it: everyone I've worked with had more in them than they'd been able or allowed to see. My job was always to help them see it.

The Philosophy

What I Believe. How I Work.

  • 🎯

    Honesty over comfort

    Growth lives on the other side of a hard conversation. I don't traffic in comfortable. I trade in honest, delivered with enough care that people can actually use it.

  • 🤝

    The work is personal, or it's nothing

    I've sat in enough workshops where nobody changed. The difference is always the same: did the facilitator actually care about this person? I do. Every time.

  • 💡

    Everyone has genius in them

    Not potential. Not promise. Actual genius, specific to who they are. My job is to help them find it, trust it, and use it. That's it.

  • 👁

    Full presence is the work

    When someone knows you're actually paying attention, not just performing, everything changes. I'm either fully in it, or I'm not there at all.

  • 🧭

    Intentional living

    I live an intentioned life, and very little happens in my world by accident. I decide who I want to be, then I work to make it real. I practice Kaizen, the Japanese philosophy of making one small improvement every single day.
